# Candidate solutions: credible hope about AI

Status: exploration, not a selected solution. September 14, 2026.

Agreed direction: build an audience by helping people feel more hopeful about AI through credible stories of its benefits. The initial audience of curious but uneasy people remains a hypothesis.

## Three reasons for hope

A short weekly edition presents three verified examples of AI benefiting people, with a source and an explicit limitation for each.

Mechanism: Repeated exposure makes beneficial developments easier to notice and recall.

Assumption: Readers want a recurring broad digest and distinguish its judgment from existing feeds.

Main risk: Appreciation does not become a habit; positive-only selection can undermine trust.

Smallest test: Hand-curate two editions for 12 prospective readers; observe requests for the next edition and later story recall.

## Meet someone AI helped

Each edition tells one real person or community story: the original difficulty, how AI was used, what changed, and what still requires human effort.

Mechanism: A concrete person and outcome make a benefit emotionally meaningful.

Assumption: A human story increases hope without appearing cherry-picked or promotional.

Main risk: Finding and verifying representative stories takes time; one anecdote can imply too much.

Smallest test: Create one sourced profile without invented quotes and test against a factual digest of the same case; measure trust, recall, and requests for more.

## Hope around what worries you

Readers choose a concern such as work, learning, or creativity and receive relevant, verified examples of helpful uses alongside unresolved limits.

Mechanism: Personal relevance connects an abstract benefit to a concern the reader actually holds.

Assumption: Useful examples address the reader's concern enough to be welcome.

Main risk: Positive examples may feel dismissive of structural harms or evade the original worry.

Smallest test: Ask six readers for their main concern, manually prepare one relevant brief each, and ask what it addressed and what it left unanswered.

## A hopeful story worth passing on

Publish a concise illustrated story or short narrated video designed for a reader to share with someone who has mixed feelings about AI.

Mechanism: A trusted person introduces the story; sharing brings new people to a recurring publication.

Assumption: Readers want to share this subject and recipients welcome the story.

Main risk: Content may circulate only among enthusiasts or be interpreted as an argument.

Smallest test: Offer a single shareable story to ten readers with no requirement to forward; track voluntary sharing and recipient interest where consent permits.

## Try one small good thing

Pair a credible benefit story with an optional ten-minute activity, such as using AI to understand a difficult concept and checking the explanation against the source.

Mechanism: A personally useful experience can create agency and grounded hope.

Assumption: A low-stakes experience changes the emotional response more than reading alone.

Main risk: Setup, errors, and privacy concerns can frustrate readers; this may become a tutorial product.

Smallest test: Compare story-only with the same story plus an optional activity; observe completion, usefulness, frustration, and desire to repeat.

## Recommended starting experiment

Test the weekly digest against a human-centered story using the same verified case. The recommendation is provisional. Start with text to keep production inexpensive; do not select a channel or build software before testing the editorial value.

A promising eventual combination is one human story plus two brief verified wins each week. Concern-led editions, sharing, and optional activities can be explored later if reader behavior supports them.

Keep observed outcomes separate from research promise and company claims. Do not imply a positive example resolves wider harms. Measure trust, specific learning, hope, and repeat demand separately.

Sources and structured assumptions are recorded in context.json. No interviews, audience tests, or new market research were conducted during solution generation.
